# GADBAD GRAH: Series Bible (Season 1)

Working title: **Gadbad Grah: Prithvi Pe Hamla** ("Planet Mess: Attack on Earth").
Written 2026-11-07. Format: Instagram Reels, 9:16, about 62 seconds each (trailer 26 s, finale 76 s), Hinglish, Pixar-style 3D. Scripts were fully rewritten on 2026-11-07 (v2): every reel has its own comedy format and an open question for the viewer.
Companion files: [SCRIPTS.md](SCRIPTS.md) (every line of dialogue), [PRODUCTION.md](PRODUCTION.md) (research, budget, test plan), [episodes.json](episodes.json) (machine-readable scripts).

---

## 1. The pitch

> A failed alien commander is sent to conquer Earth. He lands his pressure-cooker spaceship on a scooter in an Indian apartment society, and the only authority he ever meets is the society secretary, Sharma Aunty.

**The joke in one line:** the most dangerous force in the universe loses to maintenance dues, parking stickers and WhatsApp "Good Morning" groups.

**Why this is not just another alien comedy:**

1. **Aliens who learned Hindi only from Indian TV.** They speak like soap-opera villains and 90s film goons, so every threat sounds grand and every insult ("ullu ke pathe", "nalayak", "harami") comes out as a verbal tic, even when they are being polite.
2. **A mechanic you can see.** Dhamaka's power is the gem on his forehead. **Red = angry = powerful. Pink = happy = weak. Blue-white = calm = true power.** Veo cannot render text, so the rule lives in colour, not in a UI.
3. **A real secret.** The nagging ship computer, Mausi, is secretly Dhamaka's mother. The clues are planted from Reel 1 and paid off in the finale.
4. **A countdown.** "Hamle mein 12 din baaki" gives every reel a number, a reason to follow, and a deadline.

## 2. The world

### Planet Gadbad: who they are and where they came from
Told on screen in the three **Gadbad Diaries** flashbacks (P1 to P3).

**The history, in five steps:**
1. **The loud age.** Gadbad runs on **Gussa-shakti** (anger energy). Every Gadbadi has a **gem** on the forehead that glows with their mood and powers the planet's machines. Their heroes broke mountains by shouting.
2. **The boring age.** They won every war. No enemies meant no anger, no anger meant no power, and the planet's lamps started going dim.
3. **The Great Signal.** About fifteen years ago they caught Earth's TV: Indian serials, news debates, cricket fights. They watched all day and went even softer.
4. **The forgotten language.** They forgot their own language. Today every Gadbadi speaks only the Hindi they learned from TV, which is why everyone sounds like a serial villain or a news anchor.
5. **The idea.** Earth's people are angry all the time: in traffic, in family fights, in TV debates. For Gadbad, that is free power.

**How they talk:** in full dramatic TV sentences, with "Breaking news!", "Order! Order!", and threats like "main tumhe barbaad kar dunga". Small things get announced like wars.

**How they discuss:** their council runs like an Indian TV news debate. Everyone shouts at once, nobody listens, the Emperor bangs a brass bell, and the biggest decisions get made in the loudest moment.

**The people who decide:**
- **Bade Chacha** (the Emperor, a disappointed uncle) wants to save the planet. He sends Dhamaka because **nobody else volunteered**, and Tej because Tej is the real weapon.
- **Dhamaka's mother** was Gadbad's best scientist. She **vanished about a year ago** on a secret mission. Nobody talks about it. (The truth is the reveal in E14.)
- **Chacha secretly installs "Mausi"** as the ship's computer and tells Dhamaka she is a cheap new model.

### The official plan
- **Plan A (Dhamaka):** conquer Earth, keep humans permanently angry. This is the public mission.
- **Plan B (Tej):** the **Gussa-Pinjra** (anger cage) fires on Day 0 and drains the whole planet. Dhamaka is only a **decoy** to make Earth look unthreatened until then.

### Earth: Sundar Vihar Society
- A middle-class apartment society in an unnamed Indian city. Three places only (Veo handles small casts and sets):
  - **Parking lot** (night, dawn, day): hatchbacks, scooters, a guard cabin, a balcony above.
  - **Terrace**: the water tank, clotheslines, a plastic chair.
  - **Ship bridge**: the inside of the alien ship (see below).
  - **Gadbad council hall** (flashbacks only): a round hall with a golden throne, a giant old TV on the wall, and dim orange crystal lamps. The council is **heard, never seen**: off-screen shouting, two characters on screen at most.
- The rules of this universe are society rules: NOC, maintenance, fines, stickers. Aunty's power is that she knows the rules and nobody else has read them.

### The ship
A **dented orange pressure cooker with legs**. It whistles ("seeeeti") whenever it lands, powers up or panics. Inside: a cramped round cabin, riveted steel walls, three mismatched cushioned seats, a dusty CRT TV that always plays a soap, and Mausi's glowing orb on a stand.

## 3. The comedy engine

### Seven rules every script obeys
1. **Hook in the first two seconds, spoken.** A line or a visual shock, never a logo.
2. **One sentence of premise per reel.** If you cannot say it in one line, it is not ready.
3. **Status reversal.** The commander is always lower in status than the nearest aunty.
4. **Big words, small problem.** Villain-register Hindi about trivial things (parking, bills, groups).
5. **Set a pattern, then break it.** The "DHAMAKA!" line lands on a tiny "pfft" or squeak, except when it matters (E13).
6. **Every reel ends on a button or a cliffhanger**, never a fade.
7. **Nobody is humiliated for who they are.** Targets are behaviour, rank, objects and the aliens themselves (see the language guide).
8. **One laugh every shot, one new comedy format every reel.** No two reels use the same joke shape (see "Comedy formats" below).
9. **End with a question, not a request.** Bhondu asks one open question to the viewer. Never "comment 1", never "tag a friend", never "share".

### Running gags (use the same words every time so fans can quote them)
| Gag | Where it lives | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **"Ab hoga... DHAMAKA!"** then a tiny sad *pfft* | Dhamaka | Flops in E00 and E06 (the boom shrinks to a horn squeak), finally works in E13 |
| **"Pehle khaana, phir hamla."** | Mausi | Set up in E01; echoed by "sirf bhookha hai" in E05 |
| **"Sticker dikhao!"** | Aunty | Her answer to every threat: E00, E01 ("sticker kahan hai?"), then the mothership in E14 |
| **"Tumhara result kahan hai?"** (Tej vs Dhamaka) | Bade Chacha | Introduced in E05; its pain returns in the E10 betrayal |
| **"Main toh bas process follow kar raha hoon."** | Tej | Every Tej scene, including when he loses (E14) |
| **"Beta, ... Mujhe sab yaad hai."** | Mausi | Mom-guilt and mom-memory lines that slip too much (E03, E05, E08, E10) |
| **Verbal tic: "Namaste... ullu ke pathe"** | Dhamaka | Greeting + insult, he cannot stop (E02; the insult habit returns in E09) |
| **Samosa** | Bhondu | His love (E03), his gift (E04, E12), his weapon (E14) |
| **"Business ki baat hai."** | Chintu | Whenever he takes money (E02; the "customer hamesha sahi" version in E11) |
| **Gem colour change** | Dhamaka | Anger = red, joy = pink, calm = blue-white |
| **BEEP** | Mausi | Bleeps the strongest insults so the reel stays clean |

### The Hindi language guide

**How the aliens talk.** They forgot their own language during the Great Signal (told in P1), so their Hindi comes only from soap operas, news debates, 90s film villains and WhatsApp forwards. Rules: full dramatic sentences, "main tumhe barbaad kar dunga" energy, then a tiny domestic problem. Use words an Indian viewer hears on TV: *neech, nalayak, kambakht, bevakoof, gadhe, nikamme, Prithvi walon.*

**The insult ladder.** Insults are comic fuel. They must be **mild, quotable and aimed at behaviour or at each other.**

| Level | Words | Use |
|---|---|---|
| 1 (everyday) | bevakoof, nalayak, nikamma, gadhe, kambakht | Any reel, any alien |
| 2 (spicy, rationed) | **ullu ke pathe**, **harami**, haramkhor | Max 2 per reel. Dhamaka, Aunty and Tej only. Never at Chintu, Bhondu or the audience |
| 3 (strong) | anything stronger | **Never spoken.** Mausi bleeps it with a cartoon BEEP, and the bleep is the joke (E13) |

**Never write:** insults about caste, religion, region or language, body shaming, mother/sister abuse, gendered humiliation, or any real person, party or brand. The research below found that Indian AI-comedy trends lean on exactly these and are criticised for it. The goal is the opposite: **everyone is mocked equally, and Aunty always wins.**

**Voice notes (also in each `cast/<id>/character.json`):**
- **Dhamaka:** booming theatrical baritone that squeaks when flustered.
- **Bhondu:** slow, sweet sing-song, always sincere, "Sir ji..."
- **Mausi:** warm nagging-aunty voice, never rushed.
- **Sharma Aunty:** fast, sharp, fully confident, zero fear.
- **Chintu:** fast cheeky 12-year-old, all business.
- **Bade Chacha:** slow raspy elder, permanently disappointed.
- **Tej-Tarrar:** smooth smug radio-host voice, always polite.

## 4. The cast

All seven are Pixar-style 3D and each has a **draft** folder in `cast/` (descriptions are not frozen until their anchor image is accepted). Silhouette and colour differ so Veo cannot confuse them, and nobody resembles the earlier Chai Invasion cast (Commander Zog, Kamla Dadi).

### Commander Dhamaka: the hero who is a villain
- **Look:** a short round pumpkin-shaped alien, glossy orange skin, a large forehead with a glowing ruby gem, small furious black eyes under thick bushy eyebrows, two long curly whisker-feelers like a filmi villain's moustache, a black cape with a gold border.
- **Want:** to prove himself to Bade Chacha and stop being the family failure.
- **Wound:** his mother vanished a year ago. He carries her framed photo on the ship (P3) and talks to it.
- **Need:** to learn that his strength is joy, not anger.
- **Flaw:** pride. He lies, then panics.
- **Arc:** decoy and joke → reluctant society member → a friend who protects Earth.
- **Signature:** spreads his arms and shouts "Ab hoga DHAMAKA!" The gem flares red when he is angry and fades to pink when he is happy.

### Lieutenant Bhondu: the heart
- **Look:** a very tall skinny alien, soft sky-blue skin, a long noodle neck, a small round head, huge gentle glossy brown eyes, one droopy yellow-tipped antenna, a grey uniform too short at wrists and ankles.
- **Want:** one more samosa.
- **Role:** he cannot lie. He is the first alien to love Earth, and he is the reason Dhamaka learns to laugh.
- **Signature:** a samosa in hand and "Sir ji..." before every line. He also **closes most reels by asking the viewer one open question** (the 6-second closing shot).

### Mausi: the ship's computer (and a secret)
- **Look:** a floating football-sized pearl-white orb with a soft pink ring of light that pulses when she speaks. No face, no limbs. This saves production cost and avoids lip-sync problems.
- **Public role:** a nagging aunty-style ship AI. "Pehle khaana, phir hamla."
- **Secret:** she is Dhamaka's **mother**, who uploaded herself into the ship to protect him. Bade Chacha installed her and knows. The visual clue sits on the console from the start: a small framed photo of a kind orange alien woman with a **pink ring necklace**, matching Mausi's pink ring of light. Her orb is a detachable floating pod: it stays on the console until E12, where she speaks through a walkie-talkie, and **leaves the ship for the first time in E13** to protect Aunty and the kids. The reveal is in E14.
- **Clue ledger:** see section 6.

### Sharma Aunty: the real hero
- **Look:** a sturdy 50-year-old housing-society secretary, sharp black bob with one grey streak, rectangular glasses on a beaded chain, a bold magenta block-print kurta, dark red lipstick, one permanently raised eyebrow.
- **Props:** a clipboard (always), a steel thali (as a shield in E08 and E13).
- **Role:** the immovable object. She speaks in rules. She is the only person Dhamaka cannot beat.
- **Signature:** "Sticker dikhao!" and the raised eyebrow.

### Chintu: the business
- **Look:** a skinny 12-year-old, spiky black hair, a gap-toothed grin, an oversized white school shirt, blue shorts, a pencil behind his ear.
- **Role:** translator, broker and planner. He sells samosas, lessons and plans to the aliens.
- **Signature:** "Business ki baat hai." Keep him cheeky, never a victim.

### Bade Chacha: the disappointed uncle
- **Look:** an elderly alien emperor with a very large wrinkled head and a small frail body, a drooping white handlebar moustache of glowing feelers, tiny round spectacles, a ruby gem, a permanent frown, a heavy maroon robe. On Gadbad he sits on his golden throne. **On Earth he appears only as a flickering green hologram, head and shoulders**, which keeps it to one subject for Veo.
- **Role:** the Emperor, the narrator of the Gadbad Diaries, and the pressure. "Beta, result aaya?" He is not evil. He is a proud uncle who made a bad plan, and he knows who Mausi really is.

### Commander Tej-Tarrar: "Sharma-ji ka beta"
- **Look:** a tall handsome alien, glossy silver-blue skin, perfect white teeth, a sleek gold gem, slicked-back antennae, an immaculate white uniform with gold shoulders and a short white cape.
- **Role:** the cousin everyone compares Dhamaka to. Perfect, polite, and defeated by paperwork. "Main toh bas apna kaam kar raha hoon."

### The interactive engine
Every reel carries four things that invite the audience in, none of which asks for a specific word, tag or share:
1. **Bhondu's closing question** (spoken, 6 s), always open-ended and often "aur kyun?". Examples: "Aapki society ka sabse ajeeb rule kya hai?", "Aapko lagta hai Mausi kaun hai? Aur kyun?", "Aapke life ka 'Sharma ji ka beta' kaun hai?"
2. **A pinned comment** with the same question in writing (it goes under the reel, so you ask it again in your own account's voice).
3. **A hidden clue** for rewatching (the photo, the ring, a changed word) so viewers pause and hunt.
4. **A line people recognise from their own home** (mom guilt, Aunty rules, family WhatsApp group, Sharma-ji ka beta) so they send it to the person it reminds them of. This is designed in, not requested.

Three running audience games sit on top:
- **Who is Mausi?** Asked in E05, E08, E10 (guesses welcome; the answer lands in E14).
- **Dhamaka's serial dialogue class.** In E02 viewers suggest a dramatic serial-style line; the best one can be used in a later reel, with credit.
- **Aunty's Notice Board.** Viewers describe the strangest rule in their own society (E01, E04); the best ones become Aunty's fines in Season 2.

### The structure that carries a Reels series
- **Every reel stands alone** (anyone can start anywhere) **and advances the countdown** (followers want the next number).
- **Hook → premise → escalation → button** in about 50 seconds. Three beats worth remembering: the hook line, the status reversal, the closing gag.
- **Callbacks reward followers**: the pfft, "pehle khaana", "sticker dikhao", the pink gem.

## 6. Visual rules (so Veo and Omni stay consistent)

**Series style line** (paste into every shot, never change):

> Pixar-style 3D animation, warm saturated colours, soft cinematic lighting, shallow depth of field, expressive faces, no text, no captions, no subtitles, no watermarks.

- **Gem language:** red = angry, pink = happy or weak, blue-white = calm and true power. Describe it in every Dhamaka prompt.
- **No readable text in frame.** Notice-board papers, the society nameplate and the phone screens are blank or blurred. Add the title cards and captions in Vids.
- **Two characters on screen at most**, one speaker per shot. The research found multi-character lip sync is the weak spot of current video models.
- **No crowds, no vehicles at speed.** Cars are parked or crawling. Traffic is mostly sound (horns).
- **Face shots for dialogue**: medium close-up or tighter. Profile shots sync worst.
- **Mausi has no face**, so her scenes are cheap and safe. Use her to cover costly shots.

## 8. Decisions that are yours

Everything above is a complete proposal, but these four are your call:

1. **Title:** *Gadbad Grah* (my pick), *Dhamaka Commander*, or *Aunty vs Aliens*.
2. **The Mausi-is-his-mother twist:** keep it, or make her a different secret.
3. **Strength of the Hindi:** the bible keeps "ullu ke pathe" and "harami" at levels 2 and rations them. Raise or lower this.
4. **Language mix:** romanised Hinglish as written, or more English words for wider reach.
